## In the book

Near the end, high on the mountain: "Bhavi was reminded of the stories of the nymphatic apsara and the ascetic rishi that she had heard as a child."

Earlier, inside the temple in Book 3, the carving runs on without her noticing it — "friezes, mouldings, celestial dancers all continued", above a half-dozing priest by the donation box. The apsaras are on the walls the whole way through.

## In tradition

Apsaras are born of the waters and belong to Indra's court, where they dance. Their other function in the epics is blunter: when a sage's austerities grow powerful enough to threaten the gods, an apsara is sent down to end them. **Menaka** is dispatched against **Vishvamitra** and succeeds; their daughter is Shakuntala.

That is the pairing Bhavi's memory reaches for — the dancer and the ascetic — and the two of them are climbing a mountain at the time, one of them in a story about a plant that grants life.

## And yet

She remembers it as *stories she heard as a child*, which is exactly how the book tends to mark this material: not as a claim, but as something somebody was told once and never quite put down. And the version she is living through has the ascetic and the dancer walking up the same path, with a dog.
