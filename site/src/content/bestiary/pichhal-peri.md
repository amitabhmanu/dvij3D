## In the book

Second on Viraat's list, and the only creature on it the art gives more than one of: two pale, long-haired figures crouched in the undergrowth at the edge of the headlights, looking up as the van passes.

## In tradition

*Pichhal* means behind, or reversed. The pichhal peri is a woman whose feet are turned backwards, so that her tracks lead away from wherever she actually is. She belongs to the night roads of the north — the forest stretches, the river crossings, the mountain passes — and she is best known from the folklore of long-distance drivers, which is exactly who is telling the story here.

The reversed feet are not incidental. They are the standard mark of the false human all over South Asian folk belief: the thing that looks like a woman until you see which way she is going.

## And yet

"Age-old legends, fabricated to scare children from straying into forests." Viraat is a grown man driving a van, and he believes every name on his list. The Professor's line explains where such a story starts. It does not explain why it is still working on the driver.
