The *Ashtadhyayi* ("eight chapters") is the grammar of Sanskrit composed by **Panini**, probably in the 5th or 4th century BCE. Its short rules, or *sutras*, generate the forms of the language from roots (*dhatu*) and word lists (*gana*). Modern linguists still admire it as one of the most compact descriptions of a language ever written.

The Professor is an enthusiast: "An entire language etched in a handful of rules. Incredible brevity."

**From the book's notes:**

What Professor embarked upon at this stage could be termed a lucid course on the technical brevity of Ashtadhyayi...its dhatupadas and the ganapadas...that encapsulate the complete morphology of a language as vast and as ancient as Sanskrit. Unfortunately, to his less than proficient audience, it was largely indecipherable and restlessly digressive.
