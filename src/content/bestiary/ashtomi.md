## In the book

Kurup flings an arm at a hillside covered in flowering trees, high up the mountain: "Up there — that is where the Ashtomis live. A legendary race that survives by smelling flowers. They do not eat nor drink. Any unpleasant smell causes instant death."

The vignette above him draws them — four women among the blossom, each holding a flower to her face.

## In tradition

The name is a loan from a long way off. Greek accounts of India, gathered by Megasthenes and passed down through Pliny's *Natural History*, describe the **Astomi** — "the mouthless" — a people near the source of the Ganges who have no mouths, are covered in hair, and live entirely on the scent of roots, flowers and wild apples, which they carry with them on long journeys; a foul smell kills them. They travelled on into the medieval *Wonders of the East* and the monstrous-races tradition of European maps.

Indian sources have their own version of the idea: the *gandharvas*' name is heard against *gandha*, scent, and Puranic geography puts scent-eating beings in the mountains to the north, which is where Kurup is pointing.

So a race described by Greeks who had heard about India turns up in an Indian comic as a local legend of the hills. Whichever direction it travelled, it came back.

## And yet

Read what happens in the very next panel. Bhavi stops, because the water is darker at one point than elsewhere, and they find a drain pipe buried in the bank, spewing sludge into the stream. "This is why people are falling sick."

The book puts a race that dies of a bad smell directly above a page about industrial effluent. Something on this mountain really is being killed by what is in the air and the water, and it is not the Ashtomis.
