## In the book

Bhavi, halfway up the mountain and in the middle of an argument about something else, drops it on Dvij: "Though you are what my uncle would call 'a gandharva's incarnation.'"

It is left hanging. Nobody explains it, and Dvij does not ask.

## In tradition

Gandharvas are the musicians of the gods, associated with fragrance — the name is heard against *gandha*, scent — with fertility, and with the guardianship of **soma**, which they keep and which is taken from them.

They also give their name to a form of marriage. The lawbooks list eight, and the *gāndharva* marriage is the one made by mutual desire alone, with no ceremony, no priest and nobody's permission — the lovers' form, tolerated and slightly disreputable. Shakuntala and Dushyanta marry that way.

So the Professor's phrase is not a compliment about singing.

## And yet

Bhavi says it to a man who cannot remember his own name, on the same page where he dreams of the Professor explaining the five *M*s. The book is perfectly happy to let a mythological term do the work of a joke, and to leave the reader to find out what was said.
