## In the book

From the top of the fort tower the whole valley is visible, and it is strewn with enormous boulders. Bhavi has the explanation ready: "The myth says giants lived here. A legend tells of rakshasa Vraktaasura riding a mountain to fight Indra, who struck it down with his vajra. These boulders are the shattered remains."

The panel behind her draws it — the rakshasa crowned and armed on his flying mountain, the vajra coming at him in a burst of light.

## In tradition

*Rakshasas* are the night-roaming beings of the epics: shape-shifters, strong, hostile to sacrifice, and not always simply evil — Vibhishana in the *Ramayana* is a rakshasa and the story's conscience. **Vraktaasura** himself is not a figure from the texts; he belongs to this valley.

The story around him is old, though. The *Puranas* tell of mountains that once had wings and flew about the earth until Indra cut their wings off with his vajra, which is why mountains now sit still — Maināka being the one that escaped into the sea. A mountain brought down by Indra's thunderbolt, and the pieces lying where they fell, is that myth retold with a rider on it.

## And yet

Bhavi does not finish there. Two balloons later she gives the other version herself: "Or it could be that an asteroid broke apart right above, scattering pieces across the hill." The panel obliges with a meteor breaking into burning fragments over the same valley.

Then Dvij asks the question the book keeps asking: "How can we know if that is true?"
